# Football
## Decision for Accadon
Use a clock-and-event team match engine, with separate regular-time, extra-time and shoot-out scores. Academy grassroots football needs its own small-sided profiles, not merely fewer names in an eleven-a-side line-up. AIFF's 2026-27 Elite Youth League goes directly to penalties after drawn knockout matches; automatically adding extra time would be wrong. [F1, F2, F4]
Research checked 15 October 2026. Laws refer to IFAB's fixed 2026/27 edition, not an undated remembered FIFA rule. IFAB governs association-football laws; competition organisers choose permitted competition details. Futsal, beach football and informal turf competitions need separate validated profiles. [F1]
## 1. Rules, structure and win conditions
- Two teams, normally up to eleven players each including a goalkeeper. Standard association football cannot start or continue with fewer than seven, subject to the law's specified temporary off-field exceptions. Youth/grassroots modifications must be authorised in their applicable context; do not impose eleven-player or seven-minimum settings on every Blue Cubs format. [F1: law 3, notes/modifications; F2]
- Standard duration is two equal halves of 45 minutes, with a half-time interval not exceeding 15. Reduced duration must satisfy competition rules and the pre-match agreement requirement. The referee adds time lost; the indicated additional time is a minimum and may increase, not decrease. An app clock is advisory: only the referee ends a period. Extend a period for a penalty kick to be completed/retaken where required. [F1: law 7]
- A goal requires the whole ball over the goal line between the posts and beneath the crossbar, with no scoring-team offence. A goalkeeper throw directly into the opposing goal is not a goal. Most goals wins; equal goals normally mean a draw. Score provisional, confirmed and disallowed goals distinctly. [F1: law 10]
- When a winner is required, competition rules may use the law's permitted procedures, including extra time in two equal periods not exceeding 15 minutes each and penalties; away-goals rules may apply only where actually adopted. Do not infer an extra-time or away-goals rule from 'knockout'. [F1: 10.2]
- Shoot-outs occur after the match, so their kicks are not match goals. Normally each side takes five alternating kicks, ending early when the opponent cannot catch up; if level after five each, continue equal-number sudden death. Each eligible player must kick before someone kicks a second time. Keep eligible-player reductions, retakes, misses and goalkeeper changes. Warnings/cautions during the match are not carried into the shoot-out; sent-off players are ineligible. [F1: 10.3]
- The ball is out only when wholly over a touchline/goal line, the referee stops play, or a specified match-official contact requires a dropped ball. A restart type matters: throw-in, goal kick, corner, direct/indirect free kick, dropped ball and penalty are not interchangeable scoring events. A ball directly entering a goal from an indirect free kick, throw-in or dropped ball does not automatically count; apply the relevant law's restart. [F1: 8-10, 13, 15-17]
- Offside position alone is not an offence. The offence requires the involvement defined by law 11, and there is no offside offence from directly receiving a goal kick, throw-in or corner. A referee decision can invalidate a goal. Never infer offside solely from coordinates or a scorer's button. [F1: 11]
- Cautions and sendings-off are disciplinary events, not goal changes. A second caution can send off a player and affect available players; a sent-off player after kick-off is not replaced. Team officials/substitutes can also be sanctioned. Store subject type, reason, minute and resulting eligibility separately. [F1: 3, 12]
- The competition specifies permitted substitutions within IFAB limits. In relevant senior official competitions with five substitutes, there are normally three substitution opportunities plus half-time, with extra-time provisions. Return substitutions are permitted only in authorised youth/veterans/disability/grassroots contexts. Do not default return substitutions on or apply senior opportunity limits blindly to every academy match. [F1: 3]
## 2. Officiating signals and clock operations
The referee is the authoritative decision-maker. Signals in law 5 distinguish direct free kick, indirect free kick with raised arm, penalty, advantage, yellow/red card, and review/check procedures. Assistant-referee flags distinguish offside and restarts under law 6 and practical guidelines. A raised flag or an advantage signal is not itself a goal award. Capture the actual referee decision and reason; human officials confirm score changes. [F1: 5-6, practical guidelines]
The 2026/27 laws include time-related protocols and goalkeeper possession management. Under law 12, the goalkeeper's excessive hand/arm control beyond eight seconds results in a corner, with the referee visibly counting the final five seconds. Store timer warnings/restart decisions only if the adopted match profile supports them; the app must not award a corner automatically from a spectator stopwatch. [F1: 12]
Recommended match states: scheduled, team sheets submitted, checked-in, warm-up, first half, half-time, second half, regulation finished, extra-time periods if adopted, shoot-out, suspended, abandoned and confirmed. Preserve elapsed period time and display minute plus stoppage suffix, such as 45+2. A substitution during half-time should not be misdated as live first-half play. These are design recommendations derived from the laws, not extra playing rules.
## 3. Academy and coaching practice in India
AIFF Blue Cubs is for ages 4-12 with age-appropriate 2v2 through 7v7 formats, prioritising participation and enjoyment. This is a different development environment from senior eleven-a-side football. AIFF directs operators to its handbook and state-association NOC/approval route. [F2]
The 2026-27 accreditation document describes a structured youth pathway and assessment of staff, registered squads, facilities, recruitment, medical provision, grassroots activity and management. It encourages coverage of boys U13-U20 and girls U13-U18, requires relevant registered squads and grassroots provision within its accreditation scope, and recommends individual training plans over and above general team training. These are accreditation requirements/recommendations, not requirements imposed on every casual coaching business. [F3]
The same document requires an accredited academy's qualifying Blue Cubs activity to include at least one age-category league with at least eight teams over at least 14 weeks, one game per team weekly. Accadon must represent a season-long programme, not only a one-day tournament. Insurance, medical records and staff credentials need restricted access and expiry tracking. [F3]
FIFA's technical-activation session for ages 8-12 uses a technical warm-up then small-sided game application, with difficulty adapted by space, player numbers, targets and goalkeeper inclusion. Coaching questions focus on space, decision-making and transition. This supports a session/drill model with variants and observable objectives rather than just attendance. [F6]
### Proposed configurable progression and assessment
The following is a product curriculum template, not a national selection rubric.
- Foundation: movement and ball familiarity, dribbling, receiving, passing, shooting, simple cooperation and enjoyment through small-sided games. Rotate roles rather than locking a very young player into a position.
- Developing: first touch under pressure, passing options, 1v1 attack/defence, support, scanning and transitions. Use rondo/possession variants, numerical advantages and directional games chosen by the coach.
- Competitive youth: positional/unit work, pressing/build-up principles, set plays, goalkeeper-specific development and match analysis. Maintain individual plans alongside squad plans. Record maturation/injury restrictions without using chronological age as a proxy for readiness.
- Performance: opponent-specific planning, training load/recovery, role-specialised work and structured competition exposure. The pathway is academy/club and state youth competition toward national youth and senior opportunities, subject to registration and selection, never automatic promotion. [F3-F5]
Suggested session structure: readiness/warm-up, skill introduction, opposed practice, small-sided application, game/debrief. Academy chooses duration/frequency, age/ability grouping and staff/player ratios. Fitness testing, contact load and promotion thresholds require qualified local coaching decisions; no validated single India-wide default was found.
Assess technical, tactical, physical and social/behavioural dimensions separately. Suggested fields: drill/version, field dimensions, player numbers, role, task constraints, attempts/successes, observation sample, rubric and assessor/video. Examples: accurate passes under pressure, first-touch retention, scans before receiving, 1v1 decisions and recovery runs. Do not compare raw counts across unequal minutes or positions. Record goalkeeper assessments separately. Match goals alone are not a development score.
## 4. Tournament conduct and current Indian mechanics
Football competitions can use league round robin, knockout, groups into knockout and home/away or centralised phases. The 2026-27 AIFF Elite Youth League explicitly uses qualification/preliminary/zonal group phases and later final-round knockout matches. The competition regulations, not IFAB laws, define entry/qualification and league-table rules. [F4, F5]
The Elite Youth League example requires a registered squad of 20-30 players, a match list of 18 comprising eleven starters and seven substitutes, at most five substitutions without re-entry, and 90-minute matches. Its eligibility window for boys is DOB 1 January 2009 through 31 December 2011, with other specific requirements. This is an example of why age eligibility needs exact inclusive dates and exceptions, not a computed generic 'U18'. Do not apply this profile to the Junior/Sub-Junior league or all academies. [F4: 9-12]
Draw/seeding: separate team admission, geographic grouping, ranking-based seeding and random placement. No universal India-wide seed count or power-of-two draw was established. For a local knockout, configurable 8/16/32 bracket capacities and byes are proposed software support, not a claim that AIFF mandates those sizes. Persist organiser-approved group assignment, seed source and draw revision.
AIFF's youth operations manual awards three league points for a win, one for a draw, zero for loss. Equal points are resolved by head-to-head points, head-to-head goal difference, head-to-head goals, overall goal difference, overall goals, then lot. This differs from leagues that begin with overall goal difference; a generic 'football table' cannot choose one automatically. Cross-group qualification has separate appendix rules. [F5: 1]
The Elite example goes directly to penalties after drawn quarterfinals, semifinals and final; no extra time. The operations manual's ordinary forfeit provision awards a three-goal difference and three points, preserving a higher existing margin in its stated case. But the Elite regulations specify cancellation of all a withdrawn team's results in certain circumstances. Store adjudicated results and the exact applicable rule, never universally manufacture 3-0 for every abandoned fixture. [F4: 5, 12; F5: 1.3]
Scheduling: reserve a pitch and officials, allow warm-up/turnaround and team travel, avoid squad/player overlaps, track suspensions and reschedules, and respect event medical arrangements. Neither IFAB's 90 minutes nor 'one day' establishes a safe youth tournament rest rule. Academy organisers must approve daily match limits/rest and heat/rain contingencies. Publish fixtures with timezone, line-ups, period scores, final score, separate shoot-out, result status, tables, disciplinary eligibility and approved corrections. The result may change after a protest; provisional and final publication must be distinct. [F1, F4, F5]
## 5. Data model and configuration
Software recommendations derived from those requirements:
- Identity: club/academy, season, team/squad, athlete, guardian, affiliation/registration, DOB evidence, eligibility, homegrown/other entry constraints where adopted, staff credentials, medical clearance and insurance. Tenant-scope records and hide minors' evidence from public views.
- Match roster: starters, bench, goalkeeper, captain, shirt numbers, officials, team-sheet deadline/version and checks. Season roster is not match roster; substituted, temporarily off and sent-off are distinct states.
- RuleProfile: authority/edition/URL, format/players per side, minimum players, field/ball profile, period length/count, additional-time support, substitutions/opportunities/re-entry, disciplinary rules, extra-time choice, shoot-out procedure, league points/tie-break chain and forfeit/withdrawal policy.
- Match event: period and elapsed seconds, displayed minute/stoppage suffix, team/player, goal/own goal/penalty goal, disallowed/revoked, card/reason, substitution in/out, restart, suspension or correction. Keep own-goal scorer separate from credited team. Event IDs, actor, timestamp, idempotency and audit history support offline scorekeeping.
- Score aggregate: goals by period, regulation/extra-time total, shoot-out kicks/outcomes, winner and method, status and sign-off. An aggregate score for two legs is another level, not a replacement for child results.
- Table: played/won/drawn/lost, GF/GA/GD, points, deductions, head-to-head mini-table inputs, cross-group adjustment and explanation. Recompute after authoritative amendments.
- Coaching: age/ability batch, training squad, session, pitch zone, drills/constraints, staff, attendance, individual objectives, assessment rubric, training load and injury restriction. Position and sport level are time-varying observations.
Optional stats include minutes, shots/on-target, assists, saves, passes, tackles and possession. Define attribution and collection method; assists/possession are not legal scoring primitives and cannot be inferred reliably from goals.
## 6. Acceptance tests and launch gates
- A 1-1 draw followed by 4-3 penalties displays match 1-1, shoot-out 4-3, winner by penalties, not a 5-4 match. Shoot-out ends early and cycles eligible kickers correctly. [F1]
- A 45+3 goal belongs to half one. Referee-added time can increase. A disallowed goal leaves the official score unchanged. [F1]
- An Elite Youth League drawn knockout goes directly to kicks; a different approved profile may use extra time. Return-substitution mode cannot leak into the Elite profile. [F4]
- Equal-points table follows adopted head-to-head order; a withdrawal can trigger result deletion rather than ordinary forfeits. [F4, F5]
- A small-sided grassroots fixture does not inherit senior minimum players, adult period lengths or an eleven-player line-up. [F1, F2]
Before federation-grade claims, validate the profile with a referee and relevant association. Open academy choices: precise age/ability bands, session frequency, small-sided rules, rest/heat policy, selection rules, scoring-stat attribution and publication authority.
