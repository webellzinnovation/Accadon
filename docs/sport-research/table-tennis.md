# Table tennis
## Decision for Accadon
Use an event-sourced rally scorer with a table-tennis service-order engine. Store the player or pair separately from the team tie. Ship 11-point games, win by two without a cap, and configurable odd-number best-of matches. India's national-ranking example uses best of five for most rounds but best of seven for selected late rounds; one tournament-wide match length is insufficient. [T1, T3]
Research checked 15 October 2026. This is a product research and configuration brief, not a replacement for a referee or the complete laws. Rule statements below refer to ITTF 2026 Statutes chapters 2 and 3. The ITTF statutes page notes the May 2026 constitutional transition; record the adopted laws/regulations edition on each competition, and recheck later ITTF decisions before sanctioning an event. [T1, T2]
## 1. Rules and score anatomy
- Participants: singles is one player per side; doubles is an ordered pair per side. A team competition contains a series of individual singles/doubles matches, not one shared rally score. Doubles partners must alternate legal returns; service must bounce first on the server's right half-court and then the receiver's right half-court. Singles service need not be diagonal. [T1: 2.6, 2.8, 3.7.6]
- A game is won at 11 unless both sides reach 10, after which a two-point lead is required. There is no 15- or 21-point ceiling in the standard law. A match is best of any odd number of games. Store game wins independently of points. [T1: 2.11-2.12]
- A toss decides the initial choice of serve, receive or end; the opponent gets the remaining choice. Service normally changes after every two points scored in total. At 10-10, or under expedite, it changes after every point. The first server of one game receives first in the next. [T1: 2.13]
- In doubles, at a service change the previous receiver becomes server and the previous server's partner becomes receiver. The first-serving pair selects its first server in each game; after game one, the first receiver is the player who served to that first server in the previous game. Store identities, not simply a four-position counter reset blindly every game. [T1: 2.13.4-2.13.6]
- Change ends after each game. In the last possible game, change ends when the first side reaches five; in doubles the pair due to receive next changes its receiving order at that point. This is tied to the last possible game, not every game and not necessarily the fifth game. [T1: 2.13.6-2.13.7]
- Wrong server, receiver or ends: interrupt and correct the order according to the score reached; all points previously scored remain. Keep a correction event with before/after order, rather than deleting legitimate rallies. [T1: 2.14]
- A legal serve starts on the open palm, is tossed near vertically at least 16 cm, is struck on descent, remains visible and satisfies the other service requirements. A service touching the net assembly is a let if otherwise correct, or if obstructed by the receiver/pair. An unready receiver who makes no attempt to strike can also produce a let. A let repeats play without changing score or service count. A fault normally awards the opponent a point. A doubtful-service warning is not an automatic point. [T1: 2.6, 2.9-2.10]
- Other point causes include failure to make a correct return, obstruction, an opponent deliberately striking twice, touching the playing surface with the free hand, moving the playing surface, touching the net assembly, and doubles out-of-sequence play. Record the umpire's reason; do not ask software to infer contact or intent from score alone. [T1: 2.10]
- Expedite: normally introduced after 10 minutes of play in a game, or earlier at both sides' request, but not if at least 18 points have already been scored. Each server then serves one point in turn; if the receiver makes 13 correct returns in a rally, the receiver wins the point. Once introduced, expedite applies for the rest of the match. A live-rally interruption has a specific restart-server rule, so preserve server-at-interruption. [T1: 2.15]
## 2. Officiating and match operations
- The umpire calls the next server's score first, then the opponent's. The display may keep stable left/right sides, but spoken score must not simply read the left column first. At the beginning and service changes, the umpire points to the next server. The point signal is a raised closed hand on the winning side; a raised hand can indicate a let. [T1: 3.4.1]
- International-competition regulations allow up to two minutes' practice, up to one minute between games, towelling after every six points from the start of a game and at the deciding-game end change, and one timeout of up to one minute per player/pair per individual match. A timeout request is made between rallies with a T sign; it is not one timeout per game. Keep technical, injury and referee suspensions distinct from this allowance. [T1: 3.4.3-3.4.4]
- Misbehaviour: a yellow warning may precede one penalty point to the opponent for a further offence and then two for another; serious offences can go straight to the referee. Yellow plus red are shown together for penalty awards. Doubles/team carry-over rules require offender, pair and tie context; never implement cards as decorative flags. [T1: 3.5.2]
## 3. How coaching is run in India
Indian Table Tennis Academy advertises beginner, intermediate and advanced programmes, individualised training, and a dedicated fitness programme with coaches, physiotherapists and trainers. This supports level-based batches plus an individual development plan rather than an age-only ladder. It is an academy example, not a measured nationwide average. [T4]
ITTF coach education distinguishes beginners, Hopes, nationals and professionals. Its beginner material follows learn, apply and test, using verbal demonstration, visual demonstration and repetition. It explicitly teaches grip, ready position and ball-control foundations. Assessment should therefore capture performance under a stated drill, not only a coach's global star rating. [T5, T6]
### Configurable coaching progression
The following curriculum is a proposed Accadon template based on those teaching principles, not an ITTF certification standard. Coaches must approve levels, loads, thresholds and promotion decisions.
- Foundation: grip, ready position, bounce/tap control, forehand and backhand drive, basic push, legal serve, scoring and safe movement. Drills: cooperative forehand/backhand rallies, target placement and controlled feeds. Observe balance, contact, recovery and repeatability.
- Developing: two-point footwork, push-to-open patterns, topspin against backspin, block control, serve/receive placement and reading spin. Drills: multiball, regular and then random placement, third-ball patterns and constrained games. Separate forehand/backhand and consistency/decision-making assessments.
- Competitive: variable spin and pace, first-four-ball tactics, transition footwork, pressure at deuce, doubles service/receive order, opponent planning and match review. Add video clips and fitness measures chosen by a qualified coach.
- Performance: individual tactical plans, tournament periodisation, recovery, injury-managed training and specialist support. A selection pathway can run from academy matches through district/state competition to TTFI national-ranking events; do not represent this as guaranteed selection. National registration and state participation requirements must be checked with the player's association. [T3, T7]
A useful session template, proposed rather than claimed universal, is readiness/warm-up, technical repetition, movement/placement, serve-receive or tactical play, match application, then cool-down and coach notes. Duration, table-to-player ratio, feed method and weekly frequency must be academy settings; no validated single India-wide default was found.
Assessment record: drill version; feed type/spin/speed; target zone; attempts; legal successful returns; longest rally; forehand/backhand; movement pattern; rubric scores for stance/contact/recovery; video; coach; date; next goal. Repeat under comparable conditions. Keep measured counts distinct from subjective ratings and match results. Numeric pass marks and retest cadence are academy policy, not ITTF law.
## 4. Tournament mechanics and Indian example
TT supports knockout, round robin, groups feeding knockout, and team ties. For a sanctioned event, use the adopted ITTF/TTFI regulations and the current prospectus rather than assuming every event is the Olympic format. Powers of two such as 16/32/64/128 are bracket capacities, not mandatory entry counts; byes and qualifying slots occupy positions without invented match scores. [T1: 3.6-3.7, T3]
The Kanpur 2026 TTFI national-ranking prospectus is a concrete configuration example: men's/women's singles plus boys'/girls' U19, U17, U15, U13 and U11; direct main-draw entry for the top 16 men's/women's players, top eight in the other events except U11; remaining players in groups of three or four with one qualifier per group. Most matches are best of five to 11; men's/women's semifinals and finals and U19 finals are best of seven. The prospectus's clause heading says 2025 within a 2026 document: retain that source caveat and obtain referee confirmation before copying the template unchanged. [T3]
Seeding in that example prioritises men's/women's players within the ITTF world top 50, then national rankings; other events use national rankings. Seeds 1 and 2 are in opposite halves, 3-4 in prescribed opposite quarter positions, with the specified larger seed bands drawn into their allowed slots. Same-association/institution separation is specified only for first-stage groups, not later knockouts. Store ranking snapshot date, origin, seed bands and separation constraints. [T3]
Group ranking under ITTF 3.7.5 is not generic goal difference: two match points for a win, one for a played loss, zero for an unplayed/unfinished loss. For tied members, consider only their mutual results, then match points and win/loss ratios of individual matches in team events, games and points. Remove already-resolved members' matches from further tie calculations as the rule requires; unresolved equality goes to lot. Persist the calculation explanation and referee decision. Handle zero denominators explicitly rather than a database divide-by-zero. [T1: 3.7.5]
Scheduling is table-based with stage dependencies, shared-player conflicts across age events/doubles and resource availability. The Kanpur example staggers age-category qualifications and later rounds across eight days, declares its schedule tentative and gives the competition manager authority to change it. The prospectus makes venue notices/draws official and awards ranking points under TTFI guidelines. Accadon should publish a versioned draw, daily order of play, actual table assignment, match status, game-by-game scores and an approved points table. Do not invent TTFI ranking-point values. [T3]
## 5. Data model and configuration contract
These are software design recommendations derived from the rules above, not governing-body mandates.
- Shared records: academy, venue, table, coach, athlete, guardian, programme, batch, attendance, membership, development plan, assessment, competition, event/category, entry, stage, group, bracket slot, fixture, official and publication revision. Tenant-scope every identifier and preserve a single athlete across sports.
- Entrant: type player/pair/team; immutable pair membership for an entry; academy/state/institution affiliation as of entry; registration IDs; eligibility evidence and verification status; DOB and category cutoff, not just a changing age number. Protect minors' documents and contact details from public draws.
- RuleProfile: governing body, document URL, edition/effective dates, singles/doubles/team system, target 11, win-by 2, cap null, best-of by stage/round, service block 2 then 1 at deuce, deciding-game end change 5, timeout budget, intervals, expedite support and adopted exceptions.
- Match aggregate: ordered participants; initial service/receive choices; game index; game scores/winners; first server/receiver per game; live server/receiver identities; end assignment; timeouts; elapsed active-play time; expedite state; warnings/penalties; result status and confirmation.
- Rally event: monotonic sequence, rally winner, point award or let, cause, server/receiver before play, correction reference, official, timestamp and device/idempotency key. Derive score and service order by replay; undo appends a correction rather than silently rewriting history.
- TeamTie: nomination slots, tie-system template, child match order and singles/doubles discipline, clinch condition, dead-rubber policy, substitutions and approved result. Do not confuse team ties won with individual matches won.
Optional coaching/match analytics: serve success, return quality, rally length, third-ball outcome, winner/error labels and placement zones. These require explicit observation; the final score cannot establish them. Preserve denominator, coding rubric and collector.
## 6. Acceptance tests and unresolved choices
- 10-10 to 11-10 is unfinished; 12-10 is complete. 20-20 is still unfinished. A let leaves score and service unchanged. [T1]
- From 9-9, the next normal two-point service block transitions to one-point alternation once both sides reach 10. A deciding-game end change happens once at five, including best-of-three and best-of-seven profiles. Doubles receiver identity changes correctly. [T1]
- A wrong-server correction retains prior points. A penalty award can complete a game. A walkover is not a fabricated 11-0, 11-0, 11-0 played match. [T1]
- Recompute a three-way group tie using ratios of mutual results, not total point difference; reproduce the rule's removal/recalculation step. [T1]
Before a federation-grade launch, have a qualified referee validate service-order replay, expedite interruptions, team warning carry-over, walkover/retirement standings and zero-denominator tie cases. An academy may begin with best-of-five and groups-plus-knockout as a proposed local template, but the organiser must approve match lengths, qualifiers, ranking points, rest rules and publication authority.
