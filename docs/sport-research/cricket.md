# Cricket
## Decision for Accadon
Cricket requires a delivery-and-innings engine, not a points counter. Keep legal deliveries, batter runs, five extras buckets, dismissals, striker ends, innings, targets and competition adjustments separately. MCC's fourth edition of the 2017 Code took effect on 1 October 2026; ICC also publishes October 2026 format-specific playing conditions. Do not implement an old remembered law set or treat every ICC condition as mandatory for an Indian academy. [C1-C4]
Research checked 15 October 2026. The recommended first scoring scope is an organiser-approved limited-overs academy profile. Multi-day, DLS, Super Overs and federation-specific points must be explicit capabilities; hide unsupported modes rather than calculating plausible but wrong results.
## 1. Rules and score anatomy
- Standard cricket has two sides of eleven players, one captain each, with variations agreed as permitted. A match comprises one or two innings per side according to agreement/playing conditions. An innings can end through all out/no further batter available, declaration/forfeiture where allowed, allotted time/overs, or completion of the chase. Team roster size, nominated playing XI and substitutes/replacements are different concepts. [C1: 1, 13; C3: 1, 13]
- Teams score runs by batters completing valid runs, boundaries, extras and penalties. The usual boundary allowances are four and six unless agreed otherwise as allowed. A six requires a ball struck by the bat and first grounded beyond the boundary under law 19; do not count every ball over a boundary as six. [C1: 18-19]
- One over normally has six valid balls, bowled alternately from the two ends. Wides and no-balls do not count; specified dead-ball and interference cases also do not count. An umpire's miscounted over stands, so the app needs an authorised over-close override. Store integer valid-ball counts and separate delivery sequence. Display 4.3 as four overs and three balls, never the decimal number 4.3 overs. [C1: 17, 20-22]
- Score anatomy: batter runs plus no-ball extras, wides, byes, leg-byes and penalty runs. A no-ball gives a one-run penalty plus applicable runs; if hit, bat runs go to the striker. If not hit and byes/leg-byes accrue, those runs are credited appropriately, separate from the no-ball penalty. Wide-ball runs, including applicable completed runs/boundary allowance, are wides apart from separate five-run penalties. No-ball overrides wide. [C1: 18.10, 21-23]
- Leg-byes require an attempted shot or attempt to avoid being hit, as judged by the umpire. Bowler-conceded runs include bat runs, no-ball extras and wides, not ordinary byes/leg-byes. Track team extras, batter attribution and bowler attribution as separate projections. [C1: 18.10, 23]
- A dismissal needs type and dismissed batter, not just 'wicket +1'. Caught, bowled, LBW, stumped and hit-wicket can be bowler-credit dismissals; run out and the other non-bowler types must not inflate bowling wickets. On a no-ball the only permitted dismissal types are hit the ball twice, obstructing the field and run out; on a wide, the law permits hit wicket, obstructing the field, run out or stumped. Preserve umpire judgement and legal context. [C1: 21-22, 32-40]
- Runs and a wicket can coexist: on a run-out, completed runs before the wicket was fairly broken may count; the incomplete run does not. Penalties can stand even when other runs do not. Striker changes cannot be derived solely from team-total parity: boundary, dismissal, crossing and over-end rules matter. On a catch, the incoming batter takes the striker's end subject to the over-end rule, rather than following the obsolete crossed-batters convention. [C1: 18.8, 18.11-18.12, 33]
- An ordinary completed chase wins when enough runs have been scored, subject to the winning-hit/extras rules; do not keep adding later actions after the match has ended. A defending win is normally by runs; a successful chase normally by wickets remaining; multi-day can include an innings margin. Equal completed totals can be a tie. A draw is distinct from a tie; limited-overs no-result is a playing-condition outcome, not an unfinished 0-0 draw. [C1: 16; C3: 16]
- MCC 2026 clarifies overthrow versus misfield. A boundary from an overthrow includes applicable penalties, boundary allowance, completed runs at the instant of throw and the run in progress if batters had crossed then. A boundary misfield is not automatically an overthrow. Capture umpire-awarded total and classification; do not use a universal '+4 plus whatever they ran' button. [C1: 19.8; C2]
## 2. Format-specific playing conditions
ICC men's T20I October 2026 conditions use one innings each, normally a maximum 20 overs, bowler limits and field restrictions; standard ODI conditions use a different overs/fielding structure. Youth or local ten-/sixteen-over matches require their own approved limits. The law does not make every academy game T20. [C3: 13, 28; C4]
In the cited ICC T20I profile, every no-ball triggers a free hit on the following delivery; an illegal free-hit delivery carries it forward. Dismissal restrictions and field changes have specific provisions. This is an ICC limited-overs condition, not a blanket MCC free-hit law for every cricket format. [C3: 21.18]
Rain/interruptions: ICC uses DLS to calculate revised targets subject to format-specific minimum opportunity and exceptions. In the cited T20I profile, a minimum five-over framework applies unless an earlier result is achieved under the conditions. Do not invent a linear run-rate target or silently substitute a public approximation for an approved DLS calculation. Record the supplied target/par score, allocated overs, calculation source/version, time and official approval. [C3: 13, 16.4]
Tied ICC T20Is use Super Overs, with further Super Overs if tied unless the specified exception prevents completion. A Super Over is a separate one-over innings per team, ended early at two wickets; its runs are not added to the main innings or normal player totals. Local competitions may retain a tie or use another authorised rule; select the actual event policy. [C3: 16.3, appendix F]
## 3. Officiating signals and scoring workflow
MCC law 2.13 specifies: no-ball one arm horizontal; wide both arms horizontal; out index finger above head; dead ball wrists crossing below the waist; boundary four arm waved across chest; six both arms raised; bye open hand above head; leg-bye hand touching a raised knee. Five-run batting penalties use repeated opposite-hand shoulder taps; fielding penalties use a hand placed on the opposite shoulder. Revoking the last signal touches both shoulders with opposite hands; short run touches the nearer shoulder with fingertips. Signals to scorers must be separately acknowledged. [C1: 2.13, 3]
ICC additionally includes free-hit and Powerplay signals in its playing conditions. Offer composite delivery entry with review before commit: legal/illegal, bat/extras split, wicket and dismissal, completed runs, boundary/overthrow, striker/end correction, and umpire signal. A signal such as dead ball is not always synonymous with 'erase the delivery and every run'. [C1: 17-23; C3: 2.13]
Recommended workflow: draft delivery -> umpire decision -> scorer confirmation -> derived totals -> over/innings validation -> official result sign-off. Record signal revocation and score corrections as auditable events. Exceptional adjudication belongs to the umpire; an app should preserve an override and reason, not reject the authoritative result because of a simplified validator.
## 4. Coaching and player development in India
Dronacharya Cricket Academy publishes an age-linked progression: foundation 6-9 using soft balls and modified equipment, three weekly sessions; learning 9-12 with technical habits, game awareness and three/four sessions; development 13-15 with role identification, individual development plans and district competition preparation. These are that academy's age bands and frequency, not BCCI-wide requirements. [C5]
Its brochure describes intra-/inter-academy home/away matches, tournaments and a pathway through affiliated DDCA clubs. This demonstrates that coaching software needs match exposure and external club affiliation as well as paid batches. The brochure also advertises physiological/mental scans: those marketing claims are not adopted here as validated talent metrics or medical advice. [C5]
### Proposed configurable coaching template
- Foundation: grip/stance, straight-bat contact, bowling action foundations, catching/throwing and movement with age-appropriate equipment. Use play-based stations and observe enjoyment/safe technique.
- Developing: batting defence and placement, line/length bowling, fielding and wicketkeeping fundamentals; introduce rules, field settings and match situations. Separate batting, bowling, fielding and keeping assessments rather than a single level.
- Competitive: role-specific technical work, pace/spin variations, strike rotation, chasing/defending scenarios, pressure routines, fitness/recovery and match review. Store individual development plans and workload, not just runs/wickets.
- Performance: seasonal plans, specialist coaching, video and competition exposure through the appropriate district/state/club system. Registration, trials and selections remain external decisions, not an automatic software promotion. [C5-C7]
A proposed session comprises readiness/warm-up, rotating skill stations or nets, fielding/keeping, scenario match play and review/cool-down. Programme duration, player/coach/net ratio, ball type, pitch length, bowling workloads and recovery must be approved by the academy and qualified coaches. Do not fabricate universal junior bowling limits from memory.
Assessment fields: discipline/role, drill version, ball/equipment, pitch distance, feed/bowling conditions, attempts/successes, target zone, action/technique rubric, timed fielding tasks, video, assessor and development goal. Match measures need denominators: batting runs/balls, dismissals; bowling runs/valid balls/wickets; fielding chances not just catches. Test thresholds and retest schedule are proposed academy settings, not official selection standards.
## 5. Tournament mechanics, India-first
Cricket supports league, knockout, groups into knockout and super-league stages. BCCI's 2026-27 schedule is direct evidence of variation: Ranji Elite has 32 teams in four groups and quarterfinal/semifinal/final progression; Syed Mushtaq Ali Elite has four groups of eight, followed by a super league and final rather than the same quarterfinal bracket. Academy tournaments must select their actual phase graph, not copy a single 'cricket tournament' template. [C6, C7]
BCCI's current season covers men's Senior/U23/U19/U16 and women's Senior/U23/U19/U15. Other local age/skill categories vary; exact season eligibility/verification is separate from a displayed age label. State registration, age verification and nominations need the relevant current association/BCCI rules before execution. This pack does not claim to have verified every age-verification exception for every competition. [C6]
Group assignment and seeding can use prior standings, approved geographic pools or draws; no universal academy seed count exists. Store the approved method, ranking date, host/qualification slots, groups and byes. In a local knockout, 8/16/32 are useful supported capacities, not a law requiring power-of-two entry counts.
Points and tie-breaks are competition rules. ICC T20I series conditions provide one example: win two, tie/no result/abandoned one, loss zero; then wins, mutual wins and net run rate. Do not apply those points to Ranji or all BCCI events. NRR is aggregate scoring rate minus aggregate conceded rate, not the mean of per-match NRRs. All-out innings use the full entitled overs quota; no-result matches are excluded, and DLS-result matches have special credited totals/overs. Super Over contributions are not ordinary innings figures. [C3: 16.10]
Scheduling requires a ground/pitch, officials/scorers, total innings slots plus interval/turnaround, travel, weather and reserve-day policy. Multi-day fixtures need day/session calendars and stumps/resumption states. Publish fixtures, toss/line-ups, full batting/bowling/extras scorecards, fall of wickets, overs, result/margin, revised target and approved points/NRR tables. Separate local leaderboards from external BCCI selection/ranking claims. [C1, C3, C7]
## 6. Data model and configuration
Software recommendations:
- CompetitionProfile: authority/edition/URL, format, players, innings per side, overs/valid balls per over, bowler limits, field restrictions, no-ball/wide penalties, free hit, minimum result opportunity, interruption/target policy, tie/Super Over policy, points/NRR and withdrawal rules.
- Match: teams, nominated players/replacements, captain/keeper, toss and decision, venue/pitch, innings order, officials/scorers, time allocations, weather interruptions, result type/margin, approval and publication revision.
- Innings: batting/bowling side, target/par/revision history, allotted valid balls, batter order, striker/non-striker ends, bowler, total/extras, wickets, completed/all-out/declared/forfeited/chased state.
- Delivery: sequence ID separate from over.ball label, legal-ball flag, striker/non-striker/bowler, bat runs, no-ball/wide/bye/leg-bye/penalty runs, boundary flag/value, completed runs/crossing where relevant, wicket type/dismissed batter/credited bowler/fielders, free-hit state, end changes and correction reference.
- Non-delivery events: pre-delivery run-out, retired hurt/out, penalty award, target revision, replacement, over-close override, declaration, interruption and official amendment. A wicket is not always a delivered ball.
- Derived projections: team total equals bat runs plus extras; individual batting/bowling/fielding totals; partnerships; fall of wickets; legal balls/overs; run rates and NRR with decimal rates derived from integer balls. Retired hurt is not automatically a wicket; retired out is a different event.
- Academy: athlete/guardian, batches, net/ground resources, staff, attendance, discipline-specific levels, workloads/injury restrictions, assessments and individual plans. Protect health and identity evidence from public scorecards.
Optional advanced stats: wagon wheel, pitch map, shot type, speed, dropped chances and expected metrics. These require observation/sensors and clear definitions, not inference from final score.
## 7. Acceptance tests and remaining gates
- A no-ball hit for four records team +5, batter +4, no-ball extras +1, bowler +5 and no valid ball. A no-ball plus two leg-byes records +3, leg-byes +2, bowler +1 and no valid ball. [C1]
- Five wides on one delivery increase team/wide/bowler runs by five but valid balls by zero. 4.3 overs means 27 valid balls; 4.6 is not a standard display after the over closes. Umpire miscount can still stand with reason. [C1]
- Run-out plus completed runs, no-ball dismissal restrictions, catch ends, free-hit carryover and penalty-before-delivery all replay correctly. [C1, C3]
- A chase ending through a wide stops at the lawful winning event. Tie, draw and no result are distinct. NRR handles full-quota all-out and DLS adjustments. [C1, C3]
- Rain target is not calculated by a guessed linear formula; unsupported DLS is an explicit gate for official target entry/approved integration. [C3]
Qualified umpire/scorer review is needed before federation-grade scoring. Remaining competition choices: exact local playing conditions, current domestic points/eligibility, youth workloads, DLS access/approved calculator, Super Over adoption and publication authority. Research is not a claim that this brief exhausts all 42 laws and format appendices.
