# Badminton
## Decision for Accadon
Build two dated scoring profiles: current 3x21 and approved 3x15 effective 4 January 2027. Never silently migrate an ongoing tournament. Chennai's BAI 2026 circular already permits a 15-point qualifying alternative under specified approvals, so profiles must also vary by stage. Badminton and table tennis can share rally-event storage, not service-order or group-ranking algorithms. [B1, B2, B4]
Research checked 15 October 2026. Standard indoor badminton is the launch scope. AirBadminton and Para badminton require additional laws/classification; do not enable them by relabelling the standard profile. [B1, B8]
## 1. Rules and score anatomy
- Opposing sides contain one player in singles or two in doubles. Standard matches are best of three games. Every rally awards one point to its winner, including the receiving side. Current games are to 21; from 20-20 a two-point lead wins, except at 29-29 the next point wins at 30. The game winner serves first next game. [B1: definitions, 7, 10-11]
- Future profile: best of three to 15; win by two from 14-14; cap 21, so 20-20 is sudden death. Leading score eight triggers the mid-game interval and third-game end change. Between-game intervals remain 120 seconds. BWF retains 3x21 as an alternate and allows domestic organisers approved alternatives. Effective date: 4 January 2027, not October 2026. [B2]
- Toss winner chooses serve/receive or initial end; opponent gets the remaining choice. Current end changes occur after game one, after game two if a third is needed, and in game three when a side first reaches 11. A missed end change is corrected out of play; the score stands. [B1: 6, 8]
- Singles serves from right at even scores, including zero, left at odd. A server who wins scores and changes service court; a receiver who wins scores and becomes server. [B1: 10]
- Doubles: serving-side score determines right/even or left/odd. Partners change service courts only after winning a point while serving, not on a receiving-side win. The diagonally opposite player receives. Service right passes from initial server to initial receiver's partner, initial server's partner, initial receiver, and repeats. There is no second-serve life as in traditional pickleball. Either winner may serve first next game. [B1: 11]
- Wrong service order/court is corrected while the shuttle is out of play; past points stand. Preserve a correction event, not retroactive forfeiture. [B1: 12]
- Correct standard service includes diagonal courts, specified stationary foot contact, continuous forward racket movement, release without added spin, contact first with the shuttle base, and the whole shuttle below 1.15 m at impact. A missed service attempt is a fault. Do not implement the historical 'below waist' rule as the current standard profile. [B1: 9]
- Lines are in. Singles/doubles have different lateral boundaries; doubles uses a shorter rear service boundary but the full rear boundary during rallies. Net contact by a shuttle crossing legally is not by itself a service let. Faults include illegal service, out/ceiling contact, failing to cross, prohibited double hits, net contact and obstruction/invasion as defined. [B1: 1/diagram A, 9, 13-15]
- Lets include an unready receiver who does not attempt return, simultaneous server/receiver service faults, specified post-service net-entanglement cases, shuttle disintegration and defined disruptions. Play since the last service does not count; the same player serves again. [B1: 14]
## 2. Officiating and match operations
The umpire calls 'Service over' when the receiving side wins service, then the new server's score first. 'Fault', 'Let', 'Game', interval and correction calls have different effects. A line judge calls 'Out' with both arms horizontal; 'in' points toward the line with the right hand; unsighted covers the eyes with both hands. Unsighted is not a point award. Service judges use fault-specific signals; offer labelled decisions rather than automatic gesture recognition. [B3: 5, 7-8]
Current intervals are up to 60 seconds at leading score 11 and up to 120 between games. These are scheduled breaks, not player-requested timeouts. Advice is permitted while the shuttle is out of play without delaying it. Yellow is a misconduct warning; red accompanies a misconduct fault; the referee can disqualify, using the black-card procedure. Store sanctions and point consequences separately. [B1: 16-17, B3]
BWF GCR 11.4 gives a minimum 60-minute between-match interval, reducible exceptionally by the referee to no less than 30. ITTO describes 30-minute minima for junior, junior-team, Para and qualifying contexts. Preserve adopted competition scope and referee authority rather than one global rest number. Track each athlete's actual finish across all events. [B3: 3.5.19.4, B8: 11.4]
## 3. Coaching and academy practice
The Badminton Professionals, operating in Bangalore and other Indian cities, describes year-round beginner/intermediate/advanced programmes and adult/corporate training. Beginners focus on grip, footwork and warm-up/cool-down; intermediates add fitness and skill; advanced players add routines and regular match practice. Adult training distinguishes singles/doubles skills. This is documented operator practice, not a nationwide batch standard. [B5]
BWF Shuttle Time supplies a teacher manual and 22 lesson plans progressing from ten starter lessons toward more advanced skills. This supports progressive objectives and lesson versioning, not a national talent-selection rating. [B6, B7]
### Proposed Accadon curriculum
This is a configurable curriculum recommendation informed by those sources. Promotion marks, loads and frequency are academy policy.
- Foundation: grip changes, ready position, movement, serve/return, overhead preparation. Cooperative rallies, shuttle throws, target serves and controlled feeds. Assess contact, balance and recovery.
- Developing: clear/drop/lift/net control, drives, split-step, court coverage and overhead timing. Progress from predictable multi-shuttle feeds to variable feeds and constrained games.
- Competitive: first-three-shot patterns, front/back attack and side-by-side defence decisions, doubles rotation, deception, rally construction and pressure games. Keep singles/doubles tactical levels separate.
- Performance: individual plans, video/opponent review, conditioning, tournament preparation and recovery. Academy competition can lead toward district/state and BAI ranking/national events, subject to association selection and eligibility. [B4, B9]
Proposed session blocks: readiness/warm-up; movement/technical skill; fed/cooperative repetition; tactical game; match application; cool-down/reflection. Duration, court/coach ratios, feather/nylon shuttle, age band and weekly frequency must be settings. No single India-wide academy duration or promotion threshold is established by the evidence.
Assessment fields: test version, stroke, target zone, feed source, attempts/target hits, consecutive shots, footwork pattern/time if suitable, technique rubric, tactical decisions, singles/doubles role, coach/video notes and next objective. Compare equivalent feeding conditions. Do not infer technique or fitness from wins alone.
## 4. Tournament mechanics and Indian example
The Chennai 2026 senior-ranking circular provides a concrete qualifying/main-draw knockout template: MS, WS, MD, WD, XD; main draws of 64 singles and 32 doubles; ranked direct entries, host entries and qualifiers. It schedules four qualifying days then four main-draw days, publishes an M&Q list and order of play, and allows authorised 3x15 qualifying with extension to 21 for very large entries. These are event-specific, not universal BAI draw sizes. [B4]
Seed from the applicable ranking snapshot into permitted positions; store byes, qualifiers, reserves and withdrawal/promotion states. BWF regulates allowed draw changes; arbitrary reseeding after play begins is not a normal admin operation. Doubles entries need both athlete IDs and affiliations; cross-state pairs may require the circular's approvals. [B4, B8]
BWF group ranking: individual events rank by wins. A two-way tie uses head-to-head. Three-or-more-way ties use game difference, then point difference, returning to head-to-head when two remain; unresolved equality goes to lot. This is DIFFERENCE, not TT ratios. A player/pair unable to complete all group matches has all group results deleted under regulation 16.2.5. Team-event ranking separately considers ties, matches, games and points. [B8: 16]
BAI's general document lists senior, U19/U17, U15/U13 and masters categories, BAI IDs, age evidence and nomination rules. It is older/undated and some fees conflict with the Chennai 2026 notice. Use current authorised event terms for event-specific values; unresolved conflicts need clarification. Do not derive a DOB cutoff just from 'U17'. [B4, B9]
Schedule around athlete overlap across singles/doubles, partners, rest, round dependencies and courts. Distinguish scheduled, not-before, called, actual start/end. BAI standard outputs include seedings, roundwise charts, daily matches/fixtures and team nominations: support approved versioned equivalents, game-by-game results and official ranking-point tables. A local skill rating is not the BAI ranking. [B4, B10]
## 5. Data model and configuration
Software recommendations, not governing-body mandates:
- RuleProfile: authority, document URL, edition/effective dates, discipline, standard/approved alternative, games required, target/deuce/cap, interval/end-change triggers and durations, rest policy, service rule and adaptation scope.
- Entrant: athlete or immutable pair; separate pair ID and two athlete IDs; federation IDs, affiliation, eligibility/reference date and verification state. TeamTie contains nominated rubbers and clinch/dead-rubber rules.
- Match: game index, points/games, server/receiver identities, starting order, service-court mapping, ends, interval state, warnings/faults/lets, suspension, result type and official sign-off.
- Event stream: rally winner/reason, point award, service-over, let, order correction, end change, sanction, retirement/walkover and score amendment. Keep actor, timestamp, idempotency key and correction references; replay after undo/offline reconciliation.
- Tournament: discipline/category, ranking snapshot, M&Q status, qualifiers, seed constraints, bracket/byes, groups, advancement, stage-specific scoring, rest rules, order of play and publication revision.
- Coaching: programme, batch/slot, court/staff allocation, curriculum/lesson, attendance, assessment template/result, development plan and injury restriction. Athlete identity is shared across disciplines; levels need not be.
Optional coded stats: serve/receive point outcomes, rally length, shot type, errors and zones. Record collector, rubric and denominator; final scores cannot establish them.
## 6. Acceptance tests and open decisions
- Current: 21-20 unfinished; 22-20 wins; 30-29 wins. Future: 15-14 unfinished; 16-14 wins; 21-20 wins. [B1, B2]
- Receiving-side rally win scores and immediately transfers service. No second-server life. Doubles mappings survive ends/correction. [B1]
- Legal net-touch serve continues; actual let repeats without points. Wrong-court correction retains points. [B1]
- One tournament can keep authorised 15-point qualifiers and 21-point main draw; calendar changes do not mutate frozen profiles. [B2, B4]
- Group withdrawal deletion recomputes standings and qualifiers; differences are not ratios. [B8]
Have a technical official approve scoring versions, doubles replay, group deletion and domestic rest policy. Onboard academy-specific capacity, curriculum rubrics and promotion authority rather than claiming the proposal is a BWF standard.
